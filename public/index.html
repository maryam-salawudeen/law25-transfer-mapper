Where does your website send visitors' data?

Enter a website address. We'll visit it like a first-time visitor, list every company it sends data to and where they're based, and draft the privacy impact assessment Québec's Law 25 requires before personal information leaves the province.

Scan a website

A scan takes about 30 seconds to 2 minutes. We don't click the cookie banner, so you see what loads before a visitor agrees to anything. Please scan sites you're responsible for or have permission to assess.

Results

What it checks

Who gets the data

Every outside company the site contacts, matched against a list of about 100 known vendors, with where each is headquartered.

What's sent

Visitor and ad-click IDs, cookies, contact details (often hashed), email addresses, and forms that submit straight to a third party.

What Law 25 asks

Flags transfers outside Québec (s. 17) and tracking that loads before any choice (s. 8.1), then drafts the four-factor assessment for each recipient.

Good to know

  • It's not legal advice. It shows what a website sent during an automated visit, so have the results reviewed by your privacy officer.
  • Headquarters isn't where data is processed. Confirm each vendor's processing location in its data processing agreement. Other Canadian provinces count as outside Québec.
  • Before consent only. Tools that load after a visitor accepts cookies, logs in or checks out, and server-to-server transfers, aren't visible here.
  • Private by design. We don't keep the addresses you scan or the results; they go straight back to your browser. The scanner stores parameter names, never their values.
  • Want more pages or to scan privately? Run the open source command-line version on your own computer.
